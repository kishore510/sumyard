# Verification log

One row for every tax config value Sumyard holds: where it came from, when it was read, and the line it came from. A value enters `src/config/tax/<year>.ts` as a verified leaf only with a row here, in the same PR (see `CLAUDE.md` > Workflow). `src/config/tax/verification.test.ts` reads the **Values** table below and fails if it and the config disagree: one row per value, the same status, URL and date, and the same verified count per group as the Tax data screen.

Rules for every row:

- Sources are `https://www.gov.uk/` pages, fetched and read in the session that verified the value. Another site, such as legislation.gov.uk, needs an owner decision on the build's URL allowlist first (`docs/privacy.md`).
- The page must show the value for this tax year (a column or heading naming it, or a statement that it applies to it). Where a page only says "the current tax year", the row says so.
- Where two GOV.UK pages give the value, both are read; if they disagree the value stays `todo` and the conflict is logged.
- Status is `verified`, `todo` or `OPEN` (a question about how the value is applied, which the engine needs answered; the value itself may still be verified).
- Amounts are shown as the sources print them. In the config, money is integer pence and rates are exact ratios (`docs/money.md`).

Other sections log facts that are not tax config values: "Car finance (slice 3a)" holds the consumer credit conventions behind the car finance engine, "Mortgage overpayment (session M0)" the mortgage conventions, and "Next pound decision (session C0)" the pension and ISA lookups for C1 (not yet config: C1 adds them as leaves, with rows in the Values table). They have no Values table and no config leaf.

### OPEN questions

Questions about how a published figure is applied. None of them blocks a value from being verified; each blocked the engine step named. On 17 October 2026, at the start of session F, the owner answered them as modelling decisions, recorded in `docs/modelling-decisions.md` (D1 to D7 for session F's engine, G1 to G4 for session G). Each question below ends with its answer and pointer; what is still open is marked **Still OPEN**.

- **O1. National Insurance: annual or per pay period.** The config holds the annual thresholds HMRC publishes for employers (Rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027). Payroll applies weekly or monthly thresholds to each pay period (the HMRC rates page and the employers page give "£242 per week", "£1,048 per month" for the primary threshold), and twelve monthly thresholds are not the annual figure. Engine step: `employeeNI`. Decide whether Sumyard models a year of equal monthly pay (then monthly thresholds need their own leaves, one config change) or an annual approximation (then the result is labelled as one). The exact percentage method rounds per pay period (`docs/money.md`). **Answered by decision D1 (annual model):** NI is worked out per year on the annual thresholds; results say payroll works per pay period. No new leaf.
- **O2. Student loans: which pay figure, and per period or per year.** HMRC's 2026 to 2027 deduction tables say "The figure to use is the same gross pay amount that you would use to calculate your employer’s secondary Class 1 National Insurance contributions", and CSLM16030 says "the earnings figure as would be calculated for employer’s secondary Class 1 NICs. (Not income liable to tax)". The GOV.UK guide says "the amount you earn (including things like bonuses and overtime) before tax and other deductions". What this means for each pension method (salary sacrifice, net pay, relief at source) is not stated on the pages read; confirm before the engine encodes it. Deductions are per pay period and non-cumulative, with a refund if the year's income is under the yearly threshold (GOV.UK guide); Self Assessment uses the year's income. Engine step: `studentLoan`. **Answered by decision D5:** the base is NI-able pay, which matches the deduction tables' and CSLM16030's wording for session F's scope (employment income, no pension); annual, per D1. **Session G1 (GOV.UK lookup, decision G6):** answered. The base is the gross pay for employer's secondary Class 1 NICs, so salary sacrifice lowers it and net pay and relief at source leave it unchanged ("Session G1 lookups" below).
- **O3. Band boundaries.** HMRC prints bands in whole pounds ("Up to £37,700", "£37,701 to £125,140"). The config stores each band's `from` as the previous band's published upper limit and the engine is to apply a band's rate to income above it, so pence between £37,700 and £37,701 are taxed at the higher rate. Confirm. The HMRC rates page also prints the top band as "Over £125,141" where its own higher band ends at £125,140 and three other pages say "over" or "above £125,140". Engine step: `incomeTax`. **Answered by decision D2 (marginal bands),** confirmed against two published examples (`docs/worked-examples.md`, IT-1 and IT-2). The "Over £125,141" printing stays a logged printing difference.
- **O4. HICBC: partial £200 and rounding.** GOV.UK: "1% of your Child Benefit for every £200 you earn over £60,000"; PAYE14015: "for each £200 of income which falls between £60,000 and £80,000". Whether a part of £200 counts, and how the charge is rounded, is not stated on the pages read. PAYE14015's 2024-25 example gives "40% of £1,331.20" as "£453", which is not 40% of £1,331.20; its 2023-24 example gives 40% of £1,752 as "£700". Do not mirror either as a test until the owner checks them against HMRC's Child Benefit tax calculator. Engine step: `hicbc`. **Answered by decision G1 (session G):** whole steps only, following the GOV.UK guide's wording; the charge is rounded to the nearest penny at output (D3). PAYE14015's examples stay unmirrored.
- **O5. Personal allowance taper: odd pounds and pence.** "£1 for every £2" over £100,000; how half a pound (or pence) of excess is handled is not stated on the pages read. Engine step: `incomeTax`. **Answered by decision D4:** the published rule, with odd amounts settled by `ModellingPolicy.taperRounding` (`exact` by default, or `wholePoundsDown`); the two differ by less than £1 of allowance.
- **O6. Income tax rounding.** No rounding rule for income tax was found on a reachable www.gov.uk page; HMRC's tax tables (a PDF on assets.publishing.service.gov.uk) could not be fetched from this environment. Engine step: `incomeTax`. **Answered by decision D3:** no intermediate rounding; each band's tax to the nearest penny at output (`docs/money.md`).
- **O7. Childcare conditions beyond the income limit.** Both schemes test each partner's "expected adjusted net income ... for the current tax year" (over £100,000 is ineligible) and require minimum expected earnings over the next 3 months (£2,643.68 for 21 and over, £2,256.80 for 18 to 20, £1,664 under 18 or an apprentice). The schema holds only the income limit. Free Childcare for Working Parents is England only. Decide whether the optimiser models the minimum earnings and the region. Engine step: childcare cliff warnings. **Answered by decision G3 (session G):** the income-limit cliff only; minimum earnings and the England-only scope are listed assumptions. **Session G2 (G12):** both pages say "over £100,000", with no taper: a cliff, eligible at the limit; the other conditions are listed, not modelled.
- **O8. Child Benefit for a year.** Rates are weekly. HMRC's PAYE Manual (PAYE130030) counts full weeks of entitlement and multiplies by the weekly rate. How many weeks the optimiser assumes for 2026 to 2027 is not stated on the pages read. Engine step: `hicbc`. **Decision G4 (resolved by the owner in session G1):** a `ModellingPolicy` field with its default from `src/lib/periods.ts`. G1 found no page stating the count; PAYE130030's method reads as 52 or 53 full weeks for 2026 to 2027 (below). **Still OPEN for the owner:** which count; the default stands until then. **Session G2:** HMRC's press release of 31 March 2026 gives the year's amounts, each 52 times the weekly rate, matching the default ("Session G2 lookups").
- **O9. Relief at source and higher-rate relief.** GOV.UK describes higher-rate relief as a Self Assessment claim ("20% up to the amount of any income you have paid 40% tax on"; Scotland 1%, 22%, 25% and 28% at 21%, 42%, 45% and 48%), and adjusted net income deducts the grossed-up contribution ("for every £1 of pension contribution you made, take £1.25"). Whether the engine models this as a claim or as a basic-rate band extension, and the order of the ANI steps for salary sacrifice and net pay (not stated on the ANI page), need confirming. Engine step: `pensionContribution`, `adjustedNetIncome`. **Answered in part by decision G2 (session G):** relief at source as a basic rate band extension plus a reduction in adjusted net income. **Session G1 (GOV.UK lookup):** answered. Salary sacrifice and net pay contributions never count as taxable employment income (step 1); the grossed-up relief at source contribution comes off at step 3; every step is a subtraction, so the order does not change ANI (decision G2, "Session G1 lookups" below). The band extension itself is not named on any page read; it is the owner's modelling choice, tested against the claim rates the GOV.UK guide states.
- **O10. The free pay for a tax code (Tables A).** Understanding your employees' tax codes (https://www.gov.uk/employee-tax-codes) says "You usually multiply the number in the tax code by 10 to get the total amount of income they can earn before being taxed", and PAYE70025 says free pay "is a proportion of the employee’s maximum tax allowance which is reflected in the code". The figures employers use are in Tables A: Pay adjustment tables, a PDF on assets.publishing.service.gov.uk that this environment cannot fetch, so whether Tables A's free pay for a code is exactly the number times the config's `paye.codeUnit` was not checked. Engine step: `paye`. **Decision B13:** the estimate uses the number times `paye.codeUnit`, as the HTML guidance states; the assumptions say payroll can differ. Still OPEN for the owner: read Tables A.
- **O11. PAYE rounding in each pay period (Tables B to D).** PAYE70025 says Tables B to D "are used to work out tax due on an employee's taxable pay on any given pay day" and "explain the full manual method". The 2026 to 2027 tables are a PDF on assets.publishing.service.gov.uk (refused by this environment's network, as in session E), so how taxable pay to date, each band's share and the tax are rounded is not known. Engine step: `paye`. **Decision B13:** decision D3 applies (exact, each month's tax to date rounded once to the nearest penny). Still OPEN for the owner: read Tables B to D.
