The **Where does the next £X go?** page takes one lump sum of take-home pay, put in one place now, and shows what it adds to your net worth at a horizon in three places: a **pension**, a **mortgage overpayment** and an **ISA**. Each is after tax on withdrawal, in **today's money**. It is a comparison under the assumptions it lists, not advice: nothing is ranked, and nothing says which to choose. It reads this tax year's rules, so it shows when its tax figures were last verified, and shows no results while any value it needs is unverified.

## The three things you type

- **Lump sum**: take-home pay you could put in one place now. Each place spends all of it.
- **Years**: whole years from now to when each is measured.
- **Your age**: for when the pension can be taken. It is optional. Without it, the pension is shown with its numbers and the note **Access age not checked: add your age**. With it, if you couldn't take the pension by the horizon, the pension says **not accessible at that horizon**, with no number and no break-even against it.

Tap a tile to change it; it opens to a normal box.

## Where your pay and mortgage come from

- **Pay and pension** come from the [take-home page](help:take-home-pay): its inputs this visit, or a take-home scenario you saved there (see [scenarios](help:scenarios-and-saving)). The line under the title says which, with your pay and pension method. **Change** lists the take-home scenarios you have saved, newest first, and the take-home page's current inputs when it has pay. With none, the page asks you to **enter your pay on the take-home page first**, and the pension waits for it. By default it uses the take-home page's inputs this visit if they have pay, otherwise the take-home scenario saved last. The tax year comes with the pay.
- **The mortgage** comes from a mortgage scenario saved on the [mortgage page](help:mortgage), without its own overpayments: the lump sum is the one overpayment. The tile says which of your saved mortgages it is ("Saved: 1 of 2"); **Pick** lists them and **No mortgage**. By default it is the mortgage scenario saved last, or no mortgage if there is none.
- This page **only reads** them. It never changes your take-home or mortgage inputs or scenarios, and saves nothing new.
- A scenario saved here remembers which take-home and mortgage scenarios it used, and a copy of their inputs. If one has since been deleted, the page says so and uses the copy.

## The rest (closed until you open it)

- **Growth, fee and inflation**: low, mid and high growth rates for both the ISA and the pension (the starting values are illustrative, not forecasts: change them), the annual fee and inflation.
- **Pension details**: an employer match on the extra (optional), your scheme's own access age if it is later than the normal minimum age (optional), the tax rate on the pension in retirement (empty: this tax year's Basic rate band for where you live), and whether your other pensions use your lump sum allowance.
- **ISA**: what you have paid into ISAs this tax year, for the allowance warning.
- **Date the money goes in**: today, unless you change it. The access age is checked on this date plus the years.

## What you see

- **The sentence**: what the lump sum is worth in each place, in today's money at the mid growth rate, always in the order pension, mortgage, ISA.
- **Three bars** in that same order, on one shared scale so they compare. Each bar is the mid case; the thin line across it is the low to high range. Under each bar: for the pension and the ISA, the low to high range and its rates; for the mortgage, the balance with and without the overpayment and the payments no longer needed, with any early repayment charge and any cash left over. **Show the data as a table** lists every figure, in today's money and nominal.
- **What only this page tells you**: how much pension your take-home buys at your pay, per pound. Where the extra contribution brings your income back inside the band where the personal allowance is reduced, it says so.
- **The break-even box**: about what the ISA would need to return a year, before the fee, to match the mortgage overpayment and to match the pension at the mid rate. "About" is the lower end, to one decimal place. Where no rate in the range modelled matches, it says **above the highest rate modelled** or **below the lowest rate modelled**. When the mortgage result depends on the rate after the fixed period, it says so.

The bar at the top on a phone shows each place's figure at the mid rate, one line each.

## What each place assumes

- **Pension**: the largest extra contribution whose cost in take-home stays within the lump sum, worked out by the take-home model; anything it can't use is held as cash. The pot grows at the growth rates less the fee. At the horizon the tax-free part is taken (capped at the lump sum allowance available) and the rest taxed at the retirement rate. Not guaranteed.
- **Mortgage overpayment**: overpaid with the first payment, keeping the payment and shortening the term. Any early repayment charge is paid in cash from the lump sum, so the overpayment is the most whose overpayment plus charge stays within it. It is marked **Guaranteed**: it rests on the mortgage's own rates, though the rate after a fixed rate is your guess.
- **ISA**: invested at the low, mid and high rates less the fee, free of tax. Not guaranteed.

## Warnings

Warnings are always shown, grouped by place: the annual allowance, its taper and carry forward from the take-home model; the pension reaching the most the take-home model covers; the transitional rules for people close to the normal minimum pension age when it rises; an overpayment over the penalty-free limit, or more than clears the balance; and the ISA annual limit (a warning only: the ISA is not capped).

## Not modelled

The Lifetime ISA, the State Pension, a partner, offset mortgages, cash savings, inheritance tax and monthly contributions. Employer National Insurance savings. A protected pension age, benefits already taken, and the **transitional age** rules for people close to the age when it rises (a warning says when they may matter). The ISA change announced to start from the next tax year (cash held in a stocks and shares ISA). The pension search checks contributions on a finer scan, in steps of ten pounds, between the take-home model's own points, so a dip in cost narrower than that **scan step** can be passed over.

The results are worked out once you stop typing. See the assumptions on the page for every reading behind the figures.
