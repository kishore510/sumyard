The **Where does the next £X go?** page takes one lump sum of take-home pay, put in one place now, and shows what it adds to your net worth at a horizon in three columns: a **pension**, a **mortgage overpayment** and an **ISA**. Each column is after tax on withdrawal, in **today's money** first with the **nominal** amount beside it. It is a comparison under the assumptions it lists, not advice: no column is ranked, and nothing says which to choose. It reads this tax year's rules, so it shows when its tax figures were last verified, and shows no results while any value it needs is unverified.

## What you enter

- **The lump sum and you**: the lump sum, the years to the horizon, the date the money goes in (**Use today's date** fills it in) and your age now.
- **Your pay**: the same inputs as the [take-home page](help:take-home-pay): salary, bonus, where you live and the tax year. If you have entered pay on the take-home page this visit, **Copy from the take-home page** brings it all in, with benefits in kind and deductions (shown here, edited there). Nothing new is saved.
- **Your pension now**: the pension method, your contribution and your employer's, as an amount or a percentage of pensionable pay. The lump sum is added on top.
- **Pension at the horizon**: an employer match on the extra (optional), your scheme's own access age if it is later than the normal minimum age (optional), the tax rate on the pension in retirement (empty: this tax year's Basic rate band for where you live), and whether your other pensions use your lump sum allowance.
- **Annual allowance**: carry forward from earlier years and the money purchase annual allowance, as on the take-home page.
- **Mortgage (optional)**: the [mortgage page's](help:mortgage) inputs: start date, balance, term left, repayment or interest-only, the fixed rate and its end, the rate after it, an early repayment charge with its penalty-free limit, and the interest method. Left empty, the mortgage column is **not set up**: not a refusal, just not shown.
- **Growth, fee and inflation**: low, mid and high growth rates for both the ISA and the pension (the starting values are illustrative, not forecasts: change them), the annual fee and inflation.
- **ISA**: what you have paid into ISAs this tax year, for the allowance warning.

## What each column does

- **Pension**: the largest extra contribution whose cost in take-home stays within the lump sum, worked out by the take-home model; anything it can't use is held as cash in this column. The pot grows at the growth rates less the fee. At the horizon the tax-free part is taken (capped at the lump sum allowance available) and the rest taxed at the retirement rate. If the pension can't be taken by the horizon, the column says **Not accessible at horizon**, with no number and no break-even.
- **Mortgage overpayment**: the lump sum overpaid with the first payment, keeping the payment and shortening the term. Any early repayment charge is paid in cash from the lump sum, so the overpayment is the most whose overpayment plus charge stays within it. The column is the lower balance at the horizon, plus payments no longer needed up to then, plus any cash. It is marked **Guaranteed**: it rests on the mortgage's own rates, though the rate after a fixed rate is your guess.
- **ISA**: the lump sum invested at the low, mid and high rates less the fee, free of tax.

The figure always in view (on a phone, the bar at the top) is each column at the mid rate. A chart compares the three columns in today's money, with a table.

## Break-even

For the mortgage and the pension, the page says the ISA growth rate, before the fee, at which the ISA column would match that column at the horizon. Where no rate within the range modelled matches, it says the break-even is **above the highest rate modelled** or **below the lowest rate modelled**, with no number.

## Warnings

Warnings are always shown, grouped by column: the annual allowance, its taper and carry forward from the take-home model; the pension reaching the most the take-home model covers; the transitional rules for people close to the normal minimum pension age when it rises; an overpayment over the penalty-free limit, or more than clears the balance; and the ISA annual limit (a warning only: the ISA column is not capped).

## Not modelled

The Lifetime ISA, the State Pension, a partner, offset mortgages, cash savings, inheritance tax and monthly contributions. Employer National Insurance savings. A protected pension age, benefits already taken, and the **transitional age** rules for people close to the age when it rises (a warning says when they may matter). The ISA change announced to start from the next tax year (cash held in a stocks and shares ISA). The pension search checks contributions on a finer scan between the take-home model's own points, so a dip in cost narrower than that **scan step** can be passed over.

The results are worked out once you stop typing. See the assumptions on the page for every reading behind the figures.
