The **Pension contributions** chart on the [Take-home pay](help:take-home-pay) page shows what you would have to spend at each candidate pension contribution, by the method chosen in **Pension**, from nothing up to the most Sumyard models (everything you earn into the pension). It is a set of candidates, not advice: no point is called the best.

## The chart

- **Spendable** is your take-home with any Child Benefit, less any annual allowance charge. Switch to **Take-home** to see take-home alone. Childcare support is not counted in either.
- **Year or Month**: the page's **Year** / **Month** choice drives the chart, its table and everything below it. In **Month**, each figure is the year's divided by the months in a year, to the nearest penny, assuming level pay all year, and the points step by a round amount a month. Your payslips work per pay period, so for what a payslip would show, the PAYE estimate is closer.
- **The range shown**: by default the chart runs from a little below your contribution now to a little past the furthest threshold ahead of it, so the part that matters is easy to read. Switch on **Full range** to see everything from nothing to the most modelled. When no threshold lies ahead, the full range shows and the page says so. The y axis follows the points shown: it goes below nothing only when they do.
- **Numbered lines** mark your contribution now, the most modelled, and each threshold point: the smallest contribution that brings your adjusted net income (or, for the annual allowance taper, your threshold income) down to that threshold. Lines too close to number separately share one number, and the list under the chart gives every label under it. If you entered your contribution as a percentage, each marked amount also shows as a share of your pensionable pay.
- **Lettered ranges** are where the line jumps rather than moving smoothly, for example where the Child Benefit charge changes in whole steps. The line across a range is dashed: the jump lies somewhere inside it. With salary sacrifice and a student loan, the note under the chart says that repayments move in whole pounds.
- The line joins the points worked out. Between two neighbouring points the figure can move a little either way, from rounding.
- **Show the data as a table** lists every point shown, with **Change vs now** (that point's figure less the figure at your contribution now, with a sign) and what each marked point is.

## Looking at one contribution

Choose a point under **Contribution to look at**; until you do, it is your contribution now. The choice is read out above the chart, highlighted on the line and in the table, and used by the cards below. It is not saved.

- **Cost of a pound more pension** gives two figures, each with what it is measured against. **Against no contribution**: the spendable income given up for each pound going into your pension, from no contribution to the one you are looking at. **Since the previous point**: of each extra pound into your pension since the point before, how much came out of spendable income and how much did not. Into your pension means your contribution, any relief your provider adds and any salary sacrificed. Near a jump either figure can be very different from its neighbours'.
- **Thresholds from your contribution now** puts each threshold in words: **already under** it, **contribute a given amount more** (in **Month**, a month, rounded up to the penny so that much is enough), or **out of reach**, with the reason: it would take more than your pay into the pension, your pension savings would go over the annual allowance available, or your method does not lower that measure (salary sacrifice is added back to threshold income). At the Child Benefit charge's full-charge point the whole charge still applies: it first falls a penny below it.
- **Warnings across the contributions** lists every warning at any contribution, not only the range shown: over the annual allowance or the money purchase annual allowance, the tapered annual allowance, carry forward used, a childcare income limit crossed, and the rest. Each says where it applies and whether it applies to the contribution you are looking at. Warnings are never a cap: no contribution is stopped because of one.
- **Childcare**: with **Check the childcare support income limits** on (in **Pension allowances**), the warnings card says whether you are eligible on income for each scheme at the contribution you are looking at, and where you are. It is eligibility on your adjusted net income only. Not checked: a partner's income, minimum expected earnings, the child's age and circumstances, immigration status, Universal Credit or childcare vouchers, and for Free Childcare for Working Parents living in England.
- **Your contribution now and the one you are looking at** compares the two: your contribution, what goes into your pension, take-home, Child Benefit and any annual allowance charge (when either has some), spendable, all for the period, and adjusted net income for the year, each with the change.

To compare whole scenarios, save them and use compare (see [Scenarios and saving](help:scenarios-and-saving)); compare also shows carry forward, the money purchase annual allowance and the childcare check.

## Pension allowances

**Pension allowances** holds what the chart needs beyond your pay: unused annual allowance from earlier years (carry forward, as on your pension statements; it is taken as entered), whether the money purchase annual allowance applies to you, and whether to check the childcare support income limits. None of these changes your take-home.

## Not modelled

The chart is worked out for the whole year: when in the year you pay in (before or after a bonus, say) is not modelled. Also not modelled: defined benefit pension savings, income other than this job, relief claimed through Self Assessment, who pays an annual allowance charge (Scheme Pays), the value of childcare support, investment growth and tax on withdrawals. The assumptions on the page list everything, with what the optimiser does not model gathered together.
