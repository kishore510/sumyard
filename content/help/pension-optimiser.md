On the [Take-home pay](help:take-home-pay) page, **Compare a pension contribution** puts your contribution now beside one you choose, by the method chosen in **Pension**, anywhere from nothing up to the most Sumyard models (everything you earn into the pension). It shows candidates, not advice: nothing is called the best.

## Choosing a contribution

- Type an amount a year, or a percentage of pensionable pay if you enter your contribution as a percentage; or drag the slider (arrow keys move it a pound at a time); or tap **Now**, a threshold ahead of you, or **Maximum**.
- It opens on the nearest threshold ahead of your contribution now. With none ahead, it opens on now and says there is no change to compare.
- An amount above the most modelled is brought down to it, and the page says so. Contributions over your annual allowance are still shown, with the warnings; nothing is capped.
- Your choice is not saved, and it goes back to the default when you load a scenario or start again.

## Reading the answer

- One sentence says how your take-home and the total paid into your pension change, a month and a year.
- Three cards show **Now**, **New** and **Change** per month and per year, and the **% change** (on the figure now). Your contribution also shows its **share of pay** and the change in **pts** (percentage points), which is not the same as the % change. Signs are written out (+ and −); colours don't mean good or bad.
- **Total paid into your pension** is your contribution, any relief your provider adds and your employer's own contribution.
- **Spendable income** (take-home with any Child Benefit, less any annual allowance charge) has its own section, shown only when it differs from take-home.

## The chart: what each pound costs

- For each contribution from nothing to the most modelled, the take-home (with any Child Benefit, less any annual allowance charge) given up for each pound paid into your pension in total, **on average from no contribution**. It is not the cost of the next pound. The axis starts at zero.
- **Numbered lines** mark your contribution now, your target, the most modelled and each threshold point. **Lettered ranges** are where the figure jumps (for example the Child Benefit charge's whole steps); the jump lies somewhere inside. Between neighbouring points the figure can move a little either way, from rounding.
- **Show the data as a table** lists every point. **Cost detail** gives three figures, each with what it is measured on: the average from now to your target, the chart's figure at your target, and the last step before your target.

## Milestones and warnings

- **Milestones** list each threshold from your contribution now: already under it, how much more a year and a month (the month rounded up to the penny, so that much is enough), or out of reach with the reason. At the Child Benefit charge's full-charge point the whole charge still applies: it first falls a penny below it. **Milestones in detail** gives each limit and what it is measured on (adjusted net income or threshold income).
- **Warnings** at the contribution you are comparing are shown in full. A line counts them and the warnings elsewhere in the range, which are in **Warnings at other contributions**, each saying the contributions it applies at (for example "if your own contribution is £X a year or more"). Warnings are never a cap: no contribution is stopped because of one.
- **Childcare**: with **Check the childcare support income limits** on (in **Pension allowances**), the page says whether you are eligible on income for each scheme at the contribution you are comparing, and where. It is eligibility on your adjusted net income only; a partner's income, minimum expected earnings, the child's age and circumstances, immigration status, Universal Credit or childcare vouchers, and for Free Childcare for Working Parents living in England, are not checked.

To compare whole scenarios, save them and use compare (see [Scenarios and saving](help:scenarios-and-saving)); compare also shows carry forward, the money purchase annual allowance and the childcare check.

## Pension allowances

**Pension allowances** holds what the chart needs beyond your pay: unused annual allowance from earlier years (carry forward, as on your pension statements; it is taken as entered), whether the money purchase annual allowance applies to you, and whether to check the childcare support income limits. None of these changes your take-home.

## Not modelled

The chart is worked out for the whole year: when in the year you pay in (before or after a bonus, say) is not modelled. Also not modelled: defined benefit pension savings, income other than this job, relief claimed through Self Assessment, who pays an annual allowance charge (Scheme Pays), the value of childcare support, investment growth and tax on withdrawals. The assumptions on the page list everything, with what the optimiser does not model gathered together.
