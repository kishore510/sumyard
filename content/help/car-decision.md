The **Car decision** page costs up to four ways of having a car over the same stretch of time: **keep** your current car, **buy with cash**, a personal **loan**, **hire purchase** (HP) or a **PCP**. It is an illustration under your own assumptions, not advice, and it never says which option to choose: it shows which has the **lowest net cost under these assumptions**, and what would change that. It **uses no tax rules**, so it has no tax year and shows "Uses no tax rules" where other pages show when their tax figures were last verified.

## The horizon and the end value

- **Horizon**: every option is costed over the same number of months. Type it in months or years, or use the slider; years are whole years.
- **End value**: each car has three end values you enter, **low, expected and high**: what it could be worth at the end of the horizon. The switch chooses which case the cards and the order use. What a car is worth at the end is the most uncertain figure here and usually moves the answer most, so try all three.

## Shared assumptions

- **Savings return (after tax, a year)**: what your money would earn if it weren't spent on the car, after any tax on it. It is your own assumption, the same for every option. It gives the **opportunity cost**: what the cash paid out could have earned.
- **Yearly increase in running costs**: how much insurance, maintenance, VED and other costs rise each year, for every option.

Results wait until both are entered.

## Each option

Give it a name (optional) and choose how you get the car. Then fill in its sections; each header says what is entered, or what is still needed. The **optional costs and fees** (insurance, maintenance, VED, other costs, a deposit, an arrangement fee, an HP balloon, option and purchase fees, a PCP's excess mileage charge) show a 0 in an empty box: **an empty box counts as nothing**. The price, the end values, the mileage, the fuel figures, and for finance the term and the rate or payment (and a PCP's final payment and mileage allowance) are needed: until they are filled, the card lists each one.

- **The car**: its price (for keep, **What is the car worth today?**: what it would sell for today) and its **low, expected and high value at the end**, and the miles you drive a year. Between today and the end, the value falls (or rises) by the same percentage every year: an illustrative shape, not a forecast. Keeping a car counts its value as a sale given up, so keeping a car and buying the same car for the same money cost the same.
- **Finance** (loan, HP and PCP only): the loan amount or the deposit, the term in months, and either the interest rate with its basis (effective a year, or nominal with a twelfth charged each month) or the monthly payment from a quote. An arrangement fee is paid at the start or added to what you borrow. HP can have a balloon and an option to purchase fee. A PCP has an optional final payment and a purchase fee, and you choose to **buy the car** or **hand it back** at the end; handing it back pays any excess mileage over the allowance.
- **Running costs**: insurance, maintenance, **VED** and other costs a year, for the first year. VED is an amount you enter: check the rate for the car on GOV.UK, as Sumyard holds no VED figures. Fuel or energy is **petrol or diesel** (miles per gallon and the price per litre), **electric** (miles per kWh, the share charged at home, and the home and public prices) or your own **cost per mile**; the page shows the cost per mile it works out. Fuel or energy has its own yearly price change.

**Add an option** adds an empty one; **Duplicate** copies one to change a single thing; **Remove** takes one away, with **Undo**.

## The answer first

The top of the results (beside the inputs on a laptop, where it stays in view as you scroll; after the inputs on a phone) answers three questions before any detail: which option has the lowest net cost, by how much, and why.

- **Lowest net cost**: one sentence names the option with the lowest net cost under these assumptions, how much less a month it is than the next option, and how much over the horizon. It also says the savings return it uses; when that is nothing, tying up cash costs nothing in the comparison. Ties are stated. There is **no verdict** when there is only one option, when fewer than two options cover the whole horizon, or when an option that covers less (a PCP handed back early) has a lower average cost per month owned: such an option is never compared by total. On a phone the bar at the top carries the short form.
- **Switching**: when one option is **Keep current car**, a line for each other option that covers the horizon says what switching to it costs a month more or less than keeping, and over the horizon.
- **Bottom line**: the lowest net cost, the lowest **running cost** (fuel or energy, insurance, maintenance, VED and other costs) and the lowest **depreciation** (what the car loses in value), each per month owned, and what choosing the next option instead costs a month. Only options that cover the whole horizon; ties stated; each line says what it measures.
- **Low, expected and high end values**: each option's average cost per month owned in all three cases, one row each, and a sentence saying whether the option with the lowest net cost changes between them.

The order, the cards and everything below them are the detail.

## Reading the cards

- **Net cost** is the cash you pay out, less what the car is worth at the end after anything still owed on it (its **equity**), plus the opportunity cost. The headline on each card is the **average cost per month owned** (the net cost divided by the months the option covers), with the total beside it and, under it, the **largest part of the cost**.
- **Money in and out**, at the foot of each card, is the cash that changes hands, apart from the net cost: the **upfront cash** (the price for cash; the deposit and any fee paid at the start for finance; nothing for keep, whose value counts in the net cost as a sale given up), the **monthly payment** (finance only) and, for a PCP you buy at the end or HP with a balloon, the **final payment due at the end** with any fee paid with it.
- **Coverage**: an option covers the horizon unless a PCP is handed back before it ends. Then the card says "Covers only" the months of the agreement and flags it: no replacement car is modelled, so the months after are not covered. Such an option is shown per month owned and is **never ordered against options that cover the whole horizon**.
- **Equity at the horizon** is the car's value less anything still owed. If more is owed than the car is worth, the card flags **negative equity**.
- **Cash out** is everything paid over the months covered; the **breakdown** shows where the net cost goes (interest and fees, depreciation, equity handed back, fuel or energy, insurance, maintenance, VED, other and the opportunity cost), and the parts add up to the net cost. **Show as a table** lists them.
- **Order**: the cards and the list in the results column are ordered by average cost per month owned, lowest first, under these assumptions, with ties stated. Options without results yet say what they need.
- On a phone the cards are in a row that scrolls sideways; you can also move through them with Tab. **The options side by side** table below has the same figures.

## Month by month

Below the cards, a chart shows each option's **net cost so far** at the end of every month, from the start to the horizon, in the end-value case chosen: the cash paid out so far, plus any sale given up, less the equity in the car then, plus the opportunity cost so far. Each line is named at its end and drawn in its own pattern. A PCP handed back before the horizon stops at the month it goes back, marked "handed back at month …"; the horizon and each finance term are marked and listed under the chart. Where two lines cross, their running totals swap over; the order of the cards is still the one at the horizon.

**Pick a month** by tapping the chart, with the arrow keys (Home and End for the first and last month) once the chart has focus, or with a month's button in **Show the data as a table**. The card below the chart shows, for each option at that month, the net cost so far, the equity and the cash out so far, with everything else as entered. It starts at the horizon; the month you pick isn't saved with a scenario.

## What changes the answer

For two options that cover the whole horizon, the page says, once your inputs settle, the point at which their order would swap: each one's end value, your savings return, and the annual mileage **if both cars are driven the same distance, as one driver**. It starts with the two lowest average costs per month owned; choose any other two with **First option** and **Second option**. Each line changes one thing and holds everything else as entered. Where nothing in the range searched would swap them, it says **no crossing in range**. The thresholds are always measured over the whole horizon, whatever month the chart shows.

## Sensitivity: what moves the answer most

This section shows, for each option, how its average cost per month owned would change if one input moved by a fixed percentage up or down, **one at a time, with every other input held at its current value**: the end value (in the case chosen), the savings return, the annual mileage and the fuel or energy price. The inputs that move the answer most come first, with a small bar for the largest moves either way. A move that would take an input past what Sumyard models says **Outside the range**. It is worked out when you open the section, once you stop typing ("Updating" until then), since it reruns every option many times. The changes are constant percentages of today's inputs: illustrative only, not a forecast and not advice.

## Rates

For a loan, and for HP with no option to purchase fee, the card shows the **APR** worked out from the agreement's payments under the APR rules. For a PCP, and HP with an option fee, whether the APR counts the optional final payment or the fee isn't verified yet, so the card shows the **effective interest rate, not the lender’s APR**: the rate at which every payment, fees included, pays off what you borrowed. A line under it says which fees it includes: any arrangement or documentation fee, and a PCP's final payment and purchase fee, or HP's balloon and option to purchase fee.

## When there are no results

If a box isn't valid yet, it says why and only that option waits. If the figures don't work for an option (a deposit as large as the price, a loan above the price, an end value too high for the price), its card and its editor say which field and why, and show no number. The other options keep their results.

## Not modelled

Tax of any kind, VED rules, electric car tax or benefit rules, company cars, a replacement car after a hand back, ending an agreement early or settling it, part exchange, leasing, condition charges when a car is handed back, and inflation (amounts are in pounds of the day). Scenarios save the inputs, including the case shown, and compare works as on other pages (see [Scenarios and saving](help:scenarios-and-saving)). The assumptions on the page list everything.
