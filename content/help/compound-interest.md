The **Compound interest** page shows how a lump sum and regular contributions could grow at a steady rate, with an annual fee and inflation. It is an illustration, not a forecast: real returns vary from year to year and can be negative. It **uses no tax rules**, so it has no tax year and shows "Uses no tax rules" where other pages show when their tax figures were last verified.

## Your savings

- **Lump sum**: paid in once, at the start of the first year.
- **Regular contribution** and how often: weekly, monthly, quarterly or yearly.
- **Start or end of each period**: a contribution at the start of a period earns that period's growth; one at the end starts earning from the next period.
- **Years**: whole years to look ahead.

## Growth and costs

- **Growth rate** is a nominal annual rate, added at the **compounding** frequency you choose (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly or yearly). It can be negative. The page shows the **effective annual rate** beside it: what a whole year grows by once compounding is counted. When you pay in at a different frequency from the compounding, each contribution period grows at the equivalent rate, so a whole year still grows at the effective annual rate.
- **Annual fee** is a percentage of the balance a year, charged each compounding period on the balance after that period's growth. A contribution at the end of a period pays no fee for that period.

## Over time

- **Annual contribution increase**: each year's contributions are the year before's plus this percentage, starting from the second year.
- **Inflation** is used only for today's money (below).

## Results

- **Final balance**: the balance after the years you chose, with the fee. **Paid in** is the lump sum and the contributions; **growth** and **fees** are the rest.
- **Nominal or today's money**: the switch shows every figure, the chart and the yearly table either in pounds of the day (nominal) or in today's money, in prices at the start of the first year. Today's money divides each amount by inflation compounded to when it is paid in or reached, not by taking inflation off the growth rate. In today's money, growth is after the loss to inflation and can be negative.
- **What the fee costs**: the final balance without the fee and with it. **Fee drag** is the difference: the fees and the growth they would have earned, so it is more than the fees paid.
- **Rates**: the effective annual rate before fees, after fees, and after fees and inflation (the real rate).

## Chart and yearly table

The chart shows the balance by year with the fee, without it, and everything paid in so far, in the view you chose. **Show the data as a table** lists every value. The **yearly table** below the results gives each year's opening balance, paid in, growth, fees and closing balance: each row's opening, paid in and growth, less fees, make its closing balance, and the total row adds up to the final balance. On a phone it scrolls sideways with the year kept in view.

## When there are no results

If an input isn't valid yet, its box says why and results wait until it is fixed. If the balance would grow past the largest amount Sumyard can work out exactly, the page says so and shows no number: try fewer years, a lower growth rate or smaller amounts.

## Not modelled

Varying returns and ranges of outcomes, flat fees and charges on contributions, withdrawals, and any tax, ISA or pension rules. Scenarios save the inputs and compare works as on other pages (see [Scenarios and saving](help:scenarios-and-saving)). The assumptions on the page list everything.
