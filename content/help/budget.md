The **Household budget** page adds up what comes in and what goes out of your account, and shows what is left over and how much of your income goes where. It is an illustration of what you enter, not advice. It **uses no tax rules**, so it has no tax year and shows "Uses no tax rules" where other pages show when their tax figures were last verified.

## What you enter

- **Your pay** is always in view: enter it **after tax**, as it reaches your account, a month or a year. Tax, National Insurance, pension and anything else taken from your pay are not worked out here; the [Take-home pay](help:take-home-pay) page can give you a figure to enter.
- **Income extras**: any other money coming in, after any tax, each with its own period.
- Outgoings come in four kinds, each its own section:
  - **Spending**: groceries, bills, insurance and the rest.
  - **Joint account**: what you move to a joint account. It counts as going out from this account; don't enter what is spent from the joint account again.
  - **Debt**: card and loan payments. Enter the card payment or the spending on that card, **not both**, or it is counted twice.
  - **Savings and investing**: cash savings, an ISA, a fixed saver, stocks and shares. Name each line for what it is; the kind is the same.

The page starts with named lines (Groceries, Car insurance, Mobile, Other spending, Joint account, Credit card payment, Cash savings, Stocks and shares ISA, Fixed saver) as a template. A blank amount counts as nothing. Rename lines, add your own with **Add a line**, and remove any with **Remove**; a removed line comes back with **Undo**.

Each line is paid **weekly**, **monthly**, **quarterly** or **yearly**. A week counts as the weeks in a year and a quarter as the quarters, so a year is exact.

## The results

- **Left over**, with its share **of your income**, is always in view: on a phone in the bar at the top.
- **Outgoings**, **Income** and the **savings rate**: what goes to savings and investing as a share of your income. What is left over is not counted as saved.
- **Where your income goes**: a bar split into spending, joint account, debt, savings and left over, with a table.
- **By kind**: each kind's amount and share of your income.
- **Month** or **Year** switches every figure. A month is the year divided by the months, to the nearest penny, for each line and each total; so the month rows can differ from their total by a penny, and the page says so. The yearly figures add up exactly.

Every share is of your total income for the year. With no income, shares show as "n/a". When more goes out than comes in, what is left is shown below nothing, with a warning, never hidden.

## What if

In **What if**, a slider and a box for your pay and each line let you try other amounts. The results then show your inputs and the what-ifs side by side, with the change. What-ifs are **never saved**, not even in a scenario: **Reset what-ifs** drops them, and **Apply to my inputs** puts them into your inputs (with **Undo**), which you can then save.

## Not modelled

Tax, National Insurance, ISA and pension rules; interest, growth and inflation; how your budget changes over time; payslip timing (four-weekly or weekly pay months); importing from the take-home calculator. These are not modelled.
